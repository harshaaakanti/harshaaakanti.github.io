SOC automation

AI will not replace L1 SOC analysts

10 Oct 20262 min readHarshaVardhan Aakanti

Every week the same debate comes back: will generative AI make junior security analysts obsolete? My answer is no. But an analyst who works with enterprise LLMs through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) will easily outpace three analysts who refuse to adapt.

What we actually built

Over the past few months I have been modernizing our SOC workflows by connecting AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex AI and Atlassian Rovo to our security tools through MCP servers. MCP gives a model a controlled, auditable way to call tools: query the SIEM, look up an IP's reputation, pull asset context from the CMDB, read a ticket's history.

The goal was never to let a model make decisions. It was to remove the repetitive work that sits between an alert firing and a human being able to judge it.

AI isn't making decisions for us. It removes the boilerplate so analysts can make better ones.

Before and after

An L1 analyst used to stitch together telemetry from several sources by hand, check reputations, look up who owns the asset and draft the ticket. Now an automated pipeline enriches the alert in seconds, before a person opens it.

StepBeforeAfter
First-pass triageTab-switching across toolsReview of a pre-built summary
Time per alert~15 min~30 sec
False positivesReach the main queueFiltered out before the queue
Analyst energySpent clearing the queueSpent on real threat hunting

Why this protects analysts

Burnout in a SOC rarely comes from hard investigations. It comes from the hundredth identical alert of the shift. When that work is automated, junior analysts get to the interesting part of the job sooner: reasoning about intent, spotting what does not fit, and learning to hunt. That is how they become senior analysts.

What to keep in mind

  • Keep a human on every decision. The model gathers and summarizes; an analyst decides and owns the outcome.
  • Scope tool access tightly. An MCP server should expose only the read actions triage needs, with logging on every call.
  • Measure it. Track time to first decision and false-positive rate so you know the pipeline is helping, not just adding noise in a new place.

The point

The future of cyber defense is not human against machine. It is analysts with real-time context at their fingertips. If your SOC still handles L1 triage exactly the way it did two years ago, you are not only behind on tooling. You are choosing analyst burnout.
