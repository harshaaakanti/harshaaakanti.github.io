FAQ

Questions people ask

Who is HarshaVardhan Aakanti?

HarshaVardhan Aakanti is an Information Security Analyst who works on detection engineering, SOAR automation and AI-assisted triage inside an enterprise security operations centre (SOC). That work builds on three years of hunting web vulnerabilities on public and private bug bounty programs, so attacker thinking shapes how each alert gets read.

What is SOC automation?

SOC automation is the use of playbooks, scripts and integrations to handle the repetitive parts of security operations: enriching alerts, checking reputation, pulling asset context and drafting tickets. Done well, it frees analysts to spend their time on investigation and decisions. The AI-assisted triage article walks through one working pipeline.

What is detection engineering?

Detection engineering is the practice of writing, testing and tuning the rules that turn raw security telemetry into alerts. Good detections catch real attacker behaviour while producing little noise. Treating rules like software, with version control, tests and review, keeps them reliable. See detection as code and the tuning playbook.

How does AI help with SOC alert triage?

AI helps with SOC alert triage by doing the first pass of enrichment before a human looks. Large language models connected to security tools through the Model Context Protocol gather reputation, asset and history data and draft an investigation. In practice, that cut first-pass triage from about 15 minutes to about 30 seconds per alert.

Will AI replace L1 SOC analysts?

No. AI removes the boilerplate of L1 work, such as stitching telemetry together and drafting tickets, but people still make the decisions. Analysts who use AI well will outpace those who don't, and they get more time for real hunting. The full argument is in AI will not replace L1 SOC analysts.

Is it safe to use LLMs in a SOC?

LLMs are safe in a SOC only with guardrails. Alert data can carry prompt injection, and models can invent indicators of compromise that look real. Keeping a human in the loop, checking every indicator against its source and limiting what the model is allowed to do all reduce the risk. Can LLMs be trusted in the SOC? covers each one.
